Isolated kernel speedup, singlefix vs. original tuned, FULLY RANDOMIZED launch order

Same 175-point grid as this experiment's other charts, but every one of the 700,000 launches needed (5 shapes × 35 M-points × 2 configs × 2000 iterations) is generated as one flat list, shuffled into a single random order, and executed strictly in that order — bucketed back by (shape, M, config) label only after all launches complete. Every prior design here (sequential blocks, then fine-grained same-tensor interleaving) turned out to carry its own confound (block-level drift, then a confirmed cache-warming order bias); full randomization sidesteps guessing the confound's structure entirely, since no time-varying nuisance factor can correlate with a launch's label once launches from every combination are scattered uniformly across the whole run.

By far the cleanest result in this whole investigation. Null-point noise (all 173 points whose nearest anchor isn't the one that changed) collapses to stdev 0.34pp — a ~7x tighter floor than the sequential-block design's 2.43pp — with max|noise| only 1.60pp anywhere in the entire sweep. Against that floor, gate_up_proj M=2048 (anchor 2048, the only real config difference) reads +8.1%, 23.8 standard deviations from the null mean; its held-out neighbor M=2560 (same anchor) reads +4.6% (13.5 SD). Zero of the other 173 points come anywhere close. This single run is dramatically more trustworthy than any sequential-block or interleaved estimate produced earlier — though as a single run of this design, it should still be repeated a few times before treating +8.1% as final.
