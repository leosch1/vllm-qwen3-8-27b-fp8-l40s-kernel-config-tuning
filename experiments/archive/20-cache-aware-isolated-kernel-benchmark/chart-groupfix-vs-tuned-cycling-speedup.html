Isolated kernel speedup, groupfix vs. original tuned, under cache-cycling pressure

Same 175-point grid and cache-cycling methodology as this experiment's other charts, but comparing against a different baseline: the original, unpatched tuned config instead of default. Computed directly from the same absolute-microsecond numbers already logged for groupfix vs. default and tuned vs. default (both cycling runs share the identical 64-B-copy design, M grid, and hardware, so their raw durations are directly comparable without re-running anything).

groupfix only changes GROUP_SIZE_M, and only at 6 of gate_up_proj's 18 M anchors (512, 1024, 1536, 2048, 3072, 4096) — everywhere else it's byte-identical to tuned. That structure shows up directly in this chart: at every M where the two configs are literally the same, the "speedup" is just measurement noise scattered around 0% (a clean sanity check on the methodology). The real signal concentrates exactly where the parameter actually changed — +12.7% (M=512) down to +2.3% (M=2048) up to +5.3% (M=3072/4096) — confirming GROUP_SIZE_M alone, isolated from every other parameter, is what's carrying groupfix's advantage over tuned at large M on this shape.
