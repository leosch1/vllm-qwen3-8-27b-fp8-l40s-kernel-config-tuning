Isolated kernel speedup, original tuned config vs. default, under cache-cycling pressure

Same 175-point grid and cache-cycling methodology as the cache-aware/groupfix charts — this time for the plain, unpatched original tuned config (this repo's tuned-configs/, GROUP_SIZE_M=1 at every large-M anchor on gate_up_proj).

Tuned still beats default at most points, including most large-M gate_up_proj anchors (+5.8% to +9.6% at M=1024-4096) — this is not a contradiction of 14/16's GROUP_SIZE_M finding. Those experiments held every other parameter fixed and varied only GROUP_SIZE_M; tuned's other parameters (BLOCK_SIZE_M=128 vs. default's 64, num_warps=8 vs. 4) carry a real, GROUP_SIZE_M-independent advantage that outweighs GROUP_SIZE_M=1's cache-locality cost in this single-shape, no-other-confounds isolated benchmark. 14/175 points still negative overall, worst -20.5% (qkv_proj, M=160) — comparable to groupfix and cache-aware's own cycling results.

Real serving traffic (c=64, 500 requests)

Real GEMM kernel launch counts by M under actual serving traffic (from experiment 23's direct CudagraphDispatcher.dispatch() instrumentation), eager and graph-replayed launches combined into one total per M, plotted on the same log2 M axis as the per-shape speedup lines above so the two can be read together directly. Traffic is completely dominated by M=64 — the concurrency target itself — at 209,920 launches, roughly 7× the next-largest bucket (M=52, 30,208). Every M anchor the 5 shape lines above actually characterize at any density is a rounding error by comparison.

Dashed vertical lines (hover for details) mark every point above whose isolated speedup is worse than -5% — the real regressions, not the small-magnitude noise: M=40 (gate_up_proj, -15.7%), M=80 (in_proj_qkvz, -11.5%), M=160 (in_proj_qkvz -8.3% and qkv_proj -20.5%, the single worst point in the whole grid), M=768 (down_proj, -7.5%). None of these four M's carry any real traffic — every severe regression this isolated benchmark finds sits at a batch size real serving never actually hits at c=64.
