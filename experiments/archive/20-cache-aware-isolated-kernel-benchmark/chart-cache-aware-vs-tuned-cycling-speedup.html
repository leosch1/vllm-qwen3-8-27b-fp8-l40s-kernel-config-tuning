Same 175-point grid and cache-cycling methodology as this experiment's other charts, but comparing against a different baseline: the original, unpatched tuned config instead of default. Computed directly from the same absolute-microsecond numbers already logged for cache-aware vs. default and tuned vs. default (both cycling runs share the identical 64-B-copy design, M grid, and hardware, so their raw durations are directly comparable without re-running anything).

Answers a narrower question than the default-relative charts: does the cache-aware config's real retuning (not just GROUP_SIZE_M) actually beat the config it's replacing, kernel-for-kernel? Mostly yes, especially at large M on gate_up_proj (M=512: +13.6%, M=1024: +4.9%, M=2048: +5.2%) — the same anchors where the original tuned config's GROUP_SIZE_M=1 is worst. 28/175 points still negative (worst -10.7%, out_proj M=80), consistent with this being a full independent retune rather than a single-parameter patch, so it trades off differently at some M values even as it wins where it matters most.
