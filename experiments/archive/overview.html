01

Isolated kernel benchmark

confirmed, still stands — the foundation everything else reacts to or qualifies.

Measures the tuned W8A8 block-FP8 Triton kernel config against vLLM's default config at the kernel level only (one GEMM call, idle GPU), across all 5 real weight shapes, at the 18 batch sizes the tuner searched plus 17 held-out batch sizes to test generalization. Found a consistent +50–70% speedup at small M tapering to single digits by the low thousands, with 0/90 tuned anchors ever negative; held-out points were mostly clean but 6/85 went negative (worst repeat offender M=160, negative in 3/5 shapes) — a real but small (~3pp) generalization gap. Explicitly established later that this gap does NOT explain the much larger c=64 production regression found in 02 — its real contribution was ruling that hypothesis out, not confirming it.

End-to-end concurrency sweep

the anomaly is real and confirmed; the contention explanation proposed at the time was later superseded by 08/09.

Serves the real model under both tuned and default kernel configs and sweeps vllm bench serve concurrency from 1 to 128. At concurrency 1 the tuned config wins cleanly (+21–29% tok/s), but the win decays smoothly and monotonically through zero as concurrency rises, going slightly negative by c=64 (−0.8%) and c=128 (−4.1%) — a shrinking-then-reversing advantage that the kernel-level numbers alone predicted the shrinking part of but not the reversal. This is the finding that opened the rest of the investigation. A separate, more rigorous PR-evidence-style rerun was left incomplete (default rows fresh, tuned rows still from the original run).

03

GEMM traffic vs. speedup

confirmed and still stands as one piece of the picture, but the overfitting hypothesis it tested turned out not to be the main mechanism.

Instruments the live, deployed kernel dispatch code to count every real GEMM call by exact (N,K,M), to check whether real traffic actually lands on the weak M values 01 found (worst: M=160). First reading was "wrong in an interesting way": at the original --max-num-seqs=64, M=160 never occurred (a scheduler ceiling kept decode ≤64); raising to 128 let it occur — confirmed directly — yet the end-to-end delta barely moved even once the weak point was demonstrably hit. A useful negative result, not the explanation. Traffic-weighted kernel speedup was +23.2% for decode (M≤192) vs. +9.1% for prefill, explaining part (not all) of why TTFT degrades under tuning while TPOT stays comparable.

04

Contention simulation

actually run with real, important results (corrects an earlier draft that wrongly assumed it was never executed); its exact mechanism (separate OS process) was later shown to differ from the real single-process mechanism, but its central result lines up with the eventual root cause.

Reruns 01's exact default-vs-tuned kernel comparison while a separate background OS process continuously saturates the GPU with unrelated matmuls, to test whether contention explains 02's regression. At N=17408 (gate_up_proj), tuned goes sharply negative under contention at mid-to-large M (e.g. M=512: +14.7% isolated → −32.1% under contention; M=1024: +10.5% → −18.8%) while the other 4 shapes stay positive throughout — the one shape and M-range the rest of the project converges on as the real root cause, found here first via synthetic reproduction. A later profiling pass (05) established the real deployment is single-stream/single-context with zero true kernel-level simultaneity, meaning this experiment's exact mechanism (separate-process GPU time-slicing contention) differs from what's really happening in one serving process — but the fact that this cruder mechanism reproduces the same shape/M signature real serving was later found to independently produce is strong corroborating evidence, not a contradiction.

06

Instance-variance tracer artifact

confirmed, resolved — a genuine dead end that had to be ruled out explicitly.

Investigates why the very first nsys capture of the whole investigation showed tuned issuing ~11.85× more GEMM launches than default for identical traffic — large enough to fully explain the regression on its own, if real. Redeployed a second pod instance per cell and compared real CUDA API call counts (cudaGraphLaunch, cudaLaunchKernel, etc., independent of kernel-level decomposition): those counts were within 1–6% of each other across all instances — ordinary noise, nothing like the reported 11.8× gap. Root cause: a tracer decomposition artifact specific to that one first capture (~20 recorded GEMM events per CUDA-graph replay vs. ~230 per replay everywhere else), not a real execution difference. The reason for the shallower decomposition itself was not resolved. That anomalous "instance A" is excluded from every later analysis in this project.

07

GPU-metrics clock throttling

confirmed ruled out. No standalone chart — analysis was direct SQLite queries against the GPU_METRICS table, folded into 05's combined "ruled out" list.

Tests directly whether clock throttling explains the large-M gate_up_proj regression, given tuned's structurally heavier config (more shared memory/warps) is a plausible thermal/power trigger. A real clock deficit exists on the slow cluster (2376.1 vs 2511.6 MHz, −5.4%), but that's far too small to explain the ~2× real-vs-isolated gap. The decisive check: clock is lowest *before* the slow kernel starts (2295 MHz) and *rises* during and after it (2376→2433 MHz) — the opposite of self-induced throttling. With shape/config held fixed, duration barely correlates with clock at all (Pearson +0.18, and the sign runs backwards). The clincher: default shows a much *bigger* local clock dip (−19.8%) than tuned at the same shape/M, yet default is less inflated overall — if throttling drove the regression, the bigger-dip config should be more inflated, and it's the opposite. Net: throttling is a real correlate of recent heavy load, not a cause of these specific slow launches; ruling it out left the instruction/constant-cache locality hypothesis (09) as the most plausible remaining lead.

09

Isolated-vs-real FLOPs inflation

confirmed — the project's core finding, the answer to "why does c=64 regress." A caveat found later (an upstream vLLM ~10x self-timing bug in benchmark_config()) was corrected via direct nsys hardware-counter re-measurement, sidestepping rather than patching the bug.

Directly compares, at identical shape and batch size M, the same GEMM's duration measured two ways: isolated (01's method, idle GPU) vs. real (08's reverse-mapped live-serving durations), across all 5 shapes at M=1 and M=2048, to test whether FLOPs predicts the real-vs-isolated gap. After correcting the ~10x self-timing bug via hardware-counter re-measurement, the corrected picture is sharper than the original: default shows no real-vs-isolated inflation at all (real ~1181µs vs. isolated 1302.3µs — real is if anything faster); tuned shows a genuine 1.76x inflation (isolated 1169.1µs vs. real ~2057µs) — not "both configs inflate, tuned inflates more" as first thought, but "only tuned inflates, default doesn't at all." FLOPs alone doesn't cleanly predict the effect (only 5 shapes, N and K vary together) — solid for this one shape/M, inconclusive as a general law. GPU-metrics data confirms a compute-bound-to-memory-bound transition specific to tuned in real serving: Tensor Active roughly halves (58.2%→28.3%) while DRAM Read jumps ~5x (14.9%→72.8%), while default stays flat on both axes — consistent with the cache-locality framing developed across 12-14. Concludes concurrency has no direct causal role — it's a mediator that pushes real batch size M into the 640-2048 range where this one shape's tuned config degrades; the identical isolated benchmark at the identical M never shows it.

12

Cold-weight-cycling reproduction

built and run; real, correctly-directioned, but partial reproduction, confirmed directly via matched DRAM-Read/Tensor-Active metrics (v2). A follow-up ncu stall-reason probe narrows the mechanism further: it needs *sustained* repeated streaming to appear — a single isolated cache miss shows zero measurable cost for either config.

Tested whether forcing every GEMM call to read a genuinely different weight tensor (64 distinct ~89MB copies of B, matching real serving's 64 transformer layers) — rather than the same tensor repeatedly, as experiment 11's designs did — reproduces the tuned-specific slowdown. It does, partially: v2 (counterbalanced, drift-checked, n=4000/condition) found default flat under cold-cycling (1370.0µs→1369.3µs) while tuned degraded (1224.0µs→1288.4µs, +5.26%), confirmed directly in hardware counters (tuned's DRAM Read nearly doubled, 11.96%→22.46%, while default's stayed flat at ~9.2%). But tuned's cold-cycling DRAM Read (22.46%) is still far short of real serving's 72.8% — cold weight cycling is real and contributing, but insufficient alone. A follow-up single-isolated-miss ncu probe then found *zero* measurable difference for either config, pointing the mechanism toward something at the memory-controller level (DRAM row-buffer/bank contention, queue depth) that only manifests under sustained, rapid, repeated streaming — not a simple per-access cache-hit-vs-miss cost.

14

GROUP_SIZE_M surgical reproduction

built and run; the mechanism. Changing exactly one config parameter (GROUP_SIZE_M, tuned's own value only) while holding every other parameter fixed eliminates tuned's cold-weight-cycling DRAM-Read sensitivity entirely — confirmed directly via matched GPU_METRICS. The most direct, surgical evidence in the whole investigation.

Diffing default's and tuned's full config dicts surfaced GROUP_SIZE_M — Triton's L2-cache-locality grid-swizzle knob — as the outlier: default's BLOCK_SIZE_M=64 at M=2048 makes GROUP_SIZE_M=32 cover its *entire* M range (one small weight-tile resident at a time), while tuned's GROUP_SIZE_M=1 disables grouping, spreading concurrently-active blocks across many B-tiles at once. Reading the real tuning script confirmed why the tuner never saw this cost: it generates one A/B pair per shape and reuses them, unmodified, across all 1280 candidate configs in its sweep — there is no way for it to ever observe a cold-cache condition, so GROUP_SIZE_M's entire locality benefit is structurally invisible to it. Testing a third config, groupfix (tuned's exact config with only GROUP_SIZE_M changed 1→16), showed cold-cycling sensitivity vanishes: DRAM Read stayed flat (10.1%→10.2%) versus tuned's near-doubling (11.9%→22.4%), while groupfix kept almost all of tuned's speed advantage over default (1245.5µs vs. default's 1356.9µs same-B). A same-B sanity check also confirmed tuned legitimately beats groupfix (1219.5µs vs 1245.5µs, +2.1%) under the tuner's own idealized always-warm conditions — the autotuner's choice is self-consistent with what it measured, it just measured the wrong thing.

16

GROUP_SIZE_M exhaustive sweep (and its own methodology correcting itself)

built and run across four successive stages; the last stage reverses the first's headline finding. Kept as one experiment because the correction is the finding.

Set out to check whether GROUP_SIZE_M=1 really is faster at M=1024 under a rigorous, round-robin-counterbalanced sweep (stage 2: 72 conditions, 18 M-values × 4 GROUP_SIZE_M candidates) — and found it winning by 5.7%. But a fixed-tensor ncu hardware-counter probe (stage 3) flatly contradicted this, showing GROUP_SIZE_M=1 losing by 11.8% on every metric (DRAM bytes, L2 hit rate, throughput). A follow-up isolating the one remaining variable — cross-M tensor switching in stage 2's round-robin design — reversed the M=1024 result once removed (stage 4: GROUP_SIZE_M=1 loses by 1.87%), confirming stage 2's original win was a measurement artifact: round-robinning across 18 different M-values' tensors kept every condition cold, biasing the result. The correction does not undermine 14's own same-B comparison (a different, tuner-consistency question) but does suggest the real tuner's own single-pass, no-repeat, sequentially-switching methodology may itself be biased toward GROUP_SIZE_M=1 — flagged as a plausible mechanism, not separately verified.

17

Cache-aware autotune rerun

built and run across four successive stages (2-value pilot, full 5-shape/18-M rerun, 2-M noise-floor check, dedicated 10-repeat stability check); result, read together, is real and now precisely quantified.

Patches the real, unmodified benchmarks/kernels/benchmark_w8a8_block_fp8.py tuning script itself (cycling B through 64 independently-random copies to keep the L2 cache under continuous pressure during the search, rather than reusing one warm pair across all 1,280 candidates) and reruns its real exhaustive search end to end — the first experiment in this project to actually fix and rerun the tuner rather than hand-patch its output. 18

out_proj / down_proj order-based disambiguation

confirmed. Resolves >99% of the one ambiguity 08 left open, via a second, independent, purely structural method requiring no new capture or cluster access.

out_proj and down_proj share N=5120 and frequently produce byte-identical launch grid/block signatures — affecting 47.8–50.0% of every real captured GEMM launch — which 08 mostly resolved via duration-histogram bimodality but left ~3–3.5% genuinely ambiguous. This experiment tests real vLLM program order instead: within one decoder layer, kernels fire in a fixed sequence ({qkv_proj|in_proj_qkvz} → attention → out_proj → gate_up_proj → down_proj), so the always-unambiguous gate_up_proj launch anchors its neighbors as that layer's out_proj (before) and down_proj (after). Pure offline reanalysis of 05's already-captured trace data — no new cluster access needed — resolves 99.2–100% of ambiguous launches across all 4 captures, better than 08's own method, with two independent sanity checks (count ratio ≈1.0, duration ratio 2.31–2.76× vs. predicted K-ratio 2.83) both holding.

20

Cache-aware config isolated kernel benchmark

Built and run; a real, previously-unknown caveat surfaces, later refined across 11 parts.

Partly presented in the blog already. Part 11 (original tuned config under L2-flush pressure) is fully covered by main experiments/04-l2-flush-retune; Parts 1–10 are archive-only.

A 10+-part follow-up to 01's always-warm methodology, run against the cache-aware config (17 round 2), groupfix, singlefix, and the plain tuned config across always-warm, cache-cycling, and full-L2-flush regimes. Part 1 found the cache-aware config, unlike the original tuned config, does lose to default in specific mid-M spots when always-warm (worst −59.6%); Parts 2–4 showed that loss almost disappears under cache-cycling pressure (the regime that actually matters), while groupfix stays nearly spotless when warm but inherits the original tuned config's own scattered cache-pressure sensitivity. Parts 6–10 uncovered and then corrected a methodological trap — chaining two separately-run default baselines to compare two configs directly introduces noise up to −18% with zero real difference — and Part 9 confirms the real, corrected singlefix vs tuned effect at M=2048 is a repeatable +1.74% (95% CI [+1.21%, +2.27%]) via 10 independent repeats, not the raw single-pass +2.0%/+3.1pp readings. Part 11 closes the loop: under a full L2 flush (not just 64-copy cycling), the original tuned config's gate_up_proj large-M points swing sharply negative (−47.5% at M=512) exactly where GROUP_SIZE_M=1 was picked — the most dramatic direct demonstration of the regression mechanism.

Residency map

residency_map.py measures what's actually still resident in L2 after a single GEMM launch, per-slice, without a profiler — timing a probe kernel that reads one slice of A or B immediately after the GEMM, calibrated against after-flush and immediately-reread reference bandwidths, for gate_up_proj under GROUP_SIZE_M∈{1,16}. It tests two predictions: row-major should leave B roughly uniformly resident with A largely evicted; grouped should leave a residency gradient across B (oldest slices most evicted) with A resident throughout. The B results confirm both halves of the prediction: GROUP_SIZE_M=1 shows no clear gradient (0.20–0.50 across all 16 slices), while GROUP_SIZE_M=16 shows a clear gradient (0.17 at slice 0, climbing to 0.50–0.74 by slices 10–13). The A results do NOT confirm the "grouped keeps A resident" half of the prediction — resident fractions for A are near zero or negative (as low as −0.36) in BOTH configs; A looks essentially evicted regardless of ordering in this data.
