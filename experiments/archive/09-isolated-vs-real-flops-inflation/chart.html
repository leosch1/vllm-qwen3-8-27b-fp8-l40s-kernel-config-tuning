GEMM size (FLOPs) vs. how much worse real serving is than isolated benchmarking

All 5 real W8A8 shapes, at both M=1 (c=1 decode) and M=2048 (c=64 slow cluster) — 10 points total, spanning ~11,600× in FLOPs. Both isolated and real-serving durations measured live on the same hardware (isolated: isolated_all_shapes.py / isolated_all_shapes_m1.py; real: reverse-mapped from pass1-default-concurrency64-v2 / pass2-tuned-concurrency64-v2 / their c=1 counterparts). Lines connect each shape's M=1 point to its M=2048 point, within each config.

Ratio: real-serving duration ÷ isolated duration
Note this is not cleanly monotonic within a shape — tuned's own ratio for gate_up_proj is actually higher at M=1 (25.6×) than at M=2048 (17.4×), because the isolated baseline itself grows with M too. Ratio alone doesn't cleanly separate the M=1→M=2048 story; see the absolute-difference panel below for that.
Absolute difference: real-serving − isolated (µs)
The extra time each real occurrence costs versus its own isolated baseline. For 4 of 5 shapes, tuned's extra cost is consistently lower than default's at the same FLOPs. gate_up_proj is the one clear exception at M=2048, where tuned's extra cost roughly doubles default's.
Reading both panels together: the qualitative finding (gate_up_proj's tuned config is the outlier, not "big GEMMs in general") holds under both normalizations, which is reassuring. But with only 5 shapes — and N and K varying together rather than independently across them — this doesn't establish a precise functional law relating FLOPs to the effect. It shows one large, repeatable anomaly at the one shape with by far the largest N, and a roughly consistent, much smaller effect everywhere else.
