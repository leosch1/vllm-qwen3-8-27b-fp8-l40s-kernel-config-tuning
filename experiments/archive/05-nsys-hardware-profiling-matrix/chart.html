Within the reliable instances, launch count barely moves between configs at a given concurrency (536k–540k at c=64; exactly 1,310,720 at c=1, all three). What moves is mean duration — and it moves in opposite directions by concurrency: tuned is ~2.6% slower per launch at c=64, but ~27% faster per launch at c=1. That sign flip, not any change in how much work happens, is the whole story.

At c=64, GEMM's +2,187.6ms delta is over 11× larger than the next-biggest kernel's delta (silu_and_mul, −189.5ms) — and the others don't even agree on direction. Full distribution of GEMM launch durations
Every real captured GEMM launch's individual duration (not just Mean/Sum), log-spaced bins, each series normalized to % of its own launch count so default and tuned are directly comparable despite slightly different totals. Dashed lines mark each series' median (typical launch); solid lines mark its mean (what actually drives total GPU-time and TPOT).
concurrency = 64
concurrency = 1
At c=64, tuned's median launch (77.9µs) is genuinely faster than default's (86.6µs) — the bulk of the distribution shifts left, confirming the isolated-benchmark speedup is real. But the mean lines tell the opposite story (tuned 143.1µs vs. default 139.8µs) precisely because of the tail: default has a real cluster of launches around 1038–1167µs (1.63% of its launches); tuned has essentially none there, but an almost identically-sized cluster (1.64%) sits at 1864–2096µs instead — nearly the same share of launches, but ~1.8× longer when it happens, so it overcompensates the median win rather than just offsetting it. That's the concrete mechanism behind the mean/median gap: not more slow launches, just individually much slower ones.

At c=1 there's no tail to speak of in either config, and mean and median move together — tuned's distribution sits left of default's everywhere, top to bottom (mean 73.3µs vs. 100.6µs, max 237.4µs vs. 255.5µs). No contention exists at c=1 to produce a tail, so nothing erodes the improvement — a clean visual confirmation of why the same config is an unambiguous win there and a mixed result at c=64.

SM occupancy: how much of the GPU one GEMM launch actually claims
Computed from real captured launch configs (grid/block dims, registers/thread), against L40S/Ada Lovelace SM limits (65,536 registers/SM, 48 max resident warps/SM, 24 max resident blocks/SM) — not a live occupancy counter, but derived directly from what each kernel actually requested at launch, weighted by how often each shape/register variant actually fired.
concurrency = 64
concurrency = 1
At c=64, default is uniform at a flat 16.7% occupancy (128 threads/block, 243 registers/thread); tuned's dominant case (67% of launches: 256 threads/block, 110 registers/thread) reaches 33.3% — roughly double, split fairly evenly between the extra warps and a modest register-count drop.

At c=1, the gap is far bigger: default is still a flat 16.7%, but tuned's dominant case (99% of launches) reaches 83.3% — 5× default, not 2×. That can't come from doubled warps alone (warp-count doubling caps the possible gain at 2× on its own); it needs the register count to also collapse (224→48 registers/thread, a 4.7× drop) so 5 whole 8-warp blocks fit on one SM instead of 2. That register collapse traces back to a genuinely smaller tile at this M, not just more warps — the tuned config for M=1 uses BLOCK_SIZE_M=16 against default's fixed 64, wasting far less of the tile on a mostly-empty 1-real-row block. So the warps/pipeline-stages story explains most of the occupancy gain at c=64, but not at c=1 — there, the smaller tile shape (and its knock-on effect on register pressure) is doing more of the real work than the warp-count doubling by itself.
GPU-busy ratio — all runs
Total measured GPU kernel time ÷ (2 × wall-clock benchmark duration), i.e. average per-GPU utilization across the TP=2 pair.
Both configs sit in the same ~95–98% busy band at both concurrencies — c=1 is not meaningfully less saturated than c=64. Tuned's busy ratio at c=1 (94.7–95.0%) is actually very slightly lower than default's (96.1%), not higher. That's the opposite of "tuned fills the GPU better" — and it makes sense once you note this metric is normalized by wall-clock time: tuned finishes the identical real work (same 1,310,720 GEMM launches) in less wall-clock time (67.0–67.4s vs. 85.1s), because each launch is genuinely faster (73.3µs vs. 100.6µs), not because it keeps the GPU occupied a larger fraction of a similarly-long window. A single decode stream (c=1) is a tight sequential dependency chain either way — there's very little true idle time to fill in either config, so the efficiency gain shows up as a shorter total duration, not a higher busy percentage. Busy ratio measures idle-vs-not; it doesn't measure how fast the non-idle work actually was.
