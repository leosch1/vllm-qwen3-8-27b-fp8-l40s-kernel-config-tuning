The multiple peaks in the plain duration histogram are literally different GEMM shapes, not noise — and they're not even one duration per shape: gate_up_proj, M ≤128 sits around 130–140µs, while gate_up_proj, M 2048–4096 reaches 1183µs under default and 2133µs under tuned — that largest-M gate_up_proj entry is exactly the tail chart.html's mean/median-gap analysis was chasing. Now that out_proj/down_proj are resolved into their own colors (see 17) rather than one combined bucket, it's clear they see the same concurrent batch sizes as gate_up_proj — both have real, visible large-M entries too — but top out far lower: down_proj's largest-M bin reaches only ~594µs (default) / ~557µs (tuned), out_proj's only ~242µs / ~213µs. So it isn't "large M is slow" in general; every shape gets slower at large M, but only gate_up_proj turns it into a multi-hundred-percent blowout, confirming 08's finding that the regression is specific to that one weight matrix (the model's single largest, N=17408) at large M.
