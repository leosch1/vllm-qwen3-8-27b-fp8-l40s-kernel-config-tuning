Real GEMM kernel launches by M, replayed vs. eager (exact, direct measurement)

A live pod instrumented at the actual decision point (CudagraphDispatcher.dispatch(), called once per real forward step, both prefill and decode, regardless of outcome) — not inferred from gridX or graphId archaeology. Every dispatch call is logged with its exact num_tokens and resulting mode (NONE=eager, PIECEWISE/FULL=graph-replayed), then multiplied by 256 (the real per-step launch count of the GEMM kernel across all 5 shapes × their real layer counts: 64+48+16+64+64) to get exact launch counts. Real c=64 traffic, 500 requests, same benchmark parameters used throughout this project.

Startup shows the full, evenly-spaced 35-point mixed-prefill-decode capture sweep (1,2,4,...,256) plus the single eager-only M=2048 memory-profiling pass — exactly matching the source-code-derived capture-size formula. Real traffic is completely different: 84.5% of all replayed steps land on M=64 (820 of 970) — the target concurrency itself, dominating everything else combined — while the eager (red) launches are almost all large-M prefill chunks (M=312 to M=2048), confirming eager execution during real serving is prefill traffic, not decode. Notably, M=2048 recurs 33 times during real traffic (not just once at startup) — consistent with the scheduler repeatedly hitting its max_num_batched_tokens=2048 ceiling under sustained prefill load.
